### Changed
- The Terms of Service and Privacy Policy name Scoops Dev LLC, the Vermont
  company that makes PlowR, and Vermont law. "We" and "the state in which we
  are established" left the disclaimers protecting no one in particular.
- The Terms add Apple's Standard EULA, a section on the Service Report (the
  business's own record: GPS times can be late or missed, records can be
  edited, weather is an estimate, no promise a court or insurer accepts it),
  that PlowR isn't tax or legal advice, that a business is responsible for
  its clients' data and for consent to text them, and that PlowR doesn't
  vet businesses for people requesting work. The trial is "one month", as
  Apple offers it, not "30 days".
- The Privacy Policy names Apple Weather (from 1.6), says who we are and
  how to see, export or delete your data, that notifications can name
  clients, and that addresses from a scanned card or a contact go to Apple
  Maps. GitHub issues are no longer a contact for privacy questions.
- The first screen and Sign In say that continuing agrees to the Terms and
  Privacy Policy, with links. A web page saying "by using PlowR you agree"
  is often not enforced.
- The Service Report's note ends: the business's own record, not certified,
  not legal advice.
- The request page says PlowR doesn't verify businesses, links the Terms,
  and its form can't send anything anywhere if its script ever fails.
