## [1.2.0] - 2026-09-29

### Added
- **Privacy Policy link** in Settings → Legal — satisfies App Store Review Guideline 5.1.1 requirement for an in-app privacy policy link
- **Delete Account & Data** in Settings — permanently removes all local SwiftData records, the encrypted client work orders file, and Keychain credentials, then signs out; satisfies App Store Review Guideline 5.1.1(v) account deletion requirement
- **Privacy policy page** (`docs/privacy-policy.html`) hosted at `getplowr.app/privacy-policy.html` — covers all data use cases including Sign In with Apple, CloudKit sync, location/geofencing, camera/OCR, Open-Meteo, Open-Topo-Data, contacts picker, and calendar
- **Sharing an invoice or proposal has a Mark as Sent switch.** It starts on, and sharing to the client works as before: a draft invoice is marked sent, and the client shows Awaiting Response. Turn it off for a copy that isn't going to the client, such as one for a partner, an accountant or your own records, and nothing is marked. Sharing a paid invoice never marks anything, so it has no switch.

### Changed
- **PlowR runs on iOS 26.0 and later,** not only 26.5, so crews on 26.0 to 26.4 can install it. Nothing in the app needs a newer iOS: building for 26.0 proves it, because the compiler rejects any newer API used without a version check.
- **The standard services are offered, not added by themselves.** Settings → Service Catalog, and a route stop, a proposal or a client's expected services when the catalog is empty, offer Snow Removal, Lawn Care and Cleanup: one tap adds that category's common services with typical prices. PlowR used to add all eleven the first time the catalog opened, and again whenever one was missing. So a service the user deleted came back, a business that went straight to a route had no services to record and had to leave the route for Settings, and a lawn business got snow services it never asked for. Adding them by themselves also meant a second iPhone or iPad added its own set before the first device's arrived through iCloud, so every standard service showed twice. That now takes a tap on the new device before iCloud has caught up, and services already doubled that way stay until deleted by hand. A category is offered while the business has no services in it.
- **Calendar sync is a switch in Settings, off until you turn it on** (Settings → Add Visits to Calendar). Turning it on asks for full calendar access, and PlowR then keeps a "PlowR" calendar in step with the schedule for visits from the last month to six months ahead, each with a reminder an hour before. Moving or editing a visit moves its event; skipping, cancelling or deleting it removes the event; completing it keeps the event, without the reminder, as a record. Older events stay as a record. PlowR follows the schedule itself, after every change and whenever the app opens or closes, so an edit on another device or a repeat is covered too. The switch is per device; the calendar, in iCloud, shows on the user's other devices. Why: PlowR used to ask for write-only access, which can't make a calendar or put an event in one, so calendar sync never added anything (seen on a simulator: "A client with write-only access cannot move event to a new created calendar"). Had it worked, it could never have found an event again to change or remove it: skip 15 visits on a snow day and 15 reminders would still go off. The 1.1.0 notes said removals synced; nothing ever removed an event.
- The PlowR calendar goes in your iCloud calendars, so it's on your other devices, or on this iPhone if you don't use iCloud calendars. It never goes in another account such as Google or Exchange, even one with a calendar named "PlowR": the privacy policy says client details stay on the device and in iCloud. The privacy policy's calendar row says what full access is used for.
- **Add Visit offers the business's own services as reasons.** The reason list was four snow services ("Snow Plowing", "Salting / Ice Melt", "Walkway Shoveling", "Roof Snow Removal") plus "Inspection" and "Routine Visit", whatever the business did, so a lawn or landscaping crew had nothing of theirs to pick. It's now the business's services that are turned on in Settings → Service Catalog, in catalog order, then "Inspection" and "Routine Visit", then its saved reasons. A business with no services yet sees just "Inspection" and "Routine Visit" until it adds some. A visit whose reason isn't in the list any more keeps it, shown as Other.
- Two route message presets now read right for any trade. "All Clear" ("Everything will be cleared before you need to leave") is now "Almost Done" ("I'm nearly finished and will be on my way shortly."). "Door to Door" no longer promises everything "cleared".
- **"Awaiting Response" on a client's page now follows invoices and proposals sent to them.** Marking an invoice sent, or sharing any invoice or proposal to someone from the share sheet (Messages, Mail, AirDrop or another app's share option; not copying or printing it, opening it in another app such as Preview, or saving it to Photos, Files, Notes, Books or a cloud drive), shows the client as awaiting a response until you tap Mark Responded. A paid invoice shared as a receipt doesn't count. It used to be set by a text sent from the client's page, while sending a document didn't set it. Texts don't set it now, and marks left by earlier texts are cleared once, so none reads as a document sent.
- **Marking a client Inactive takes them off their routes.** Inactive clients were already left out of new routes, but stayed on the routes they were on. If they're on any, PlowR now says which and asks first. Marking them active again doesn't put them back. Clients already marked Inactive come off their routes once, the first time this version opens.
- **Marking an invoice paid ends "Awaiting Response"**: a payment is the client's response.
- **Sharing a draft invoice to someone marks it sent**, with its due date, as Mark Sent does. That includes a new invoice shared from its preview and saved from that same preview. Going back to the form makes a new copy, which is saved as a draft; so is an invoice whose number another invoice took meanwhile, as it's saved with the next number. Share or Mark Sent either one again. It used to stay a draft, with no due date running, though the client had it.
- **Upcoming visits follow their client too.** A changed name or address now reaches the client's visits still ahead on the schedule, and their events in the "PlowR" calendar, as it already reaches their route stops. That includes today's visits, done or not, and the next visit a repeating series adds when one is completed; visits on earlier days keep theirs, as a record. A visit used to keep the address it was booked with, so a client who moved was still due at their old house.
- **Adjust Pin suggests the address it finds instead of taking it.** After you move the pin, the address the map finds there is shown with "Use This Address"; the client's address changes only if you tap it, and it's offered only when it's a different address, not the same one written another way. It used to be replaced on every move, often by a reformatted version of the same address or a neighbour's number. A new address typed on the client's page before moving the pin is the one shown and kept, and it's saved with the moved pin; if it was picked from the address suggestions, the map opens at its pin rather than the old one.
- **When a changed address can't be found, a client who has a pin keeps it or gets the address typed again.** The prompt offers "Keep Current Pin" (the new address with the client's pin, which Adjust Pin can move) or "Edit Address". A client with no pin is still offered "Save Without Pin". The same choice is offered when the map couldn't be reached, alongside Try Again.

### Fixed
- **A route in progress survives the app closing.** The current stop, the stop timer and the Live Activity lived inside the route screen, and leaving that screen ended the route. If iOS killed the app mid-route (low memory, or swiped away), the route was gone: back to stop 1, the Live Activity orphaned on the Lock Screen. The route now lives in `ActiveRouteStore`, which saves a small checkpoint (route, stop, when that stop started) on every change and restores it at launch. The app reopens straight into the route at the same client and takes over its Live Activity rather than starting a second one (tested at store level; not yet run on a device). The current stop is kept by identity, so a route reordered on another device doesn't move you to a different client, and a route deleted on another device ends cleanly instead of being driven after it's gone. If the stop on screen was changed on another device before you tap Complete or Notify, nothing is recorded and a "Route Changed" message asks you to check the current stop, so a visit is never credited to a client who wasn't visited. The notify prompt stays addressed to the client it opened for. A newly current stop (after a sync change or a relaunch) starts its own timer. Only End Route finishes a route. Completing the last stop now shows "All stops complete" instead of leaving the last stop's Complete Route button live, so it can't record the visit twice.
- **Deleting your account also clears the home-screen widget's copy of today's route** (route and client names) and ends any route in progress. Found while adding the route checkpoint, which the deletion now also removes.
- **Saving a job photo no longer needs a permission the app never asked for.** The photo share sheet offers Save Image, which needs `NSPhotoLibraryAddUsageDescription`; without it iOS stops the app. The string is now in `Info.plist`.
- **Invoices from a route no longer bill a client once per mapped zone.** Record Services shows one price for a per-square-foot service: the rate times the client's whole mapped area. Saving wrote that whole-property price onto every zone's line, so a client with two zones was billed twice and one with three zones three times, without anyone typing anything. The price is now split across the zones in proportion to their area, in whole cents, so the lines always add up to the figure the driver saw. A client with one zone, or none, was never affected.
- **Duplicating or revising a document keeps every line's amount.** Copies recomputed each line from quantity × unit price, which silently undid any amount typed over the catalog price, including edits made on the invoice's Edit screen.
- **"Group line items by service" keeps the total.** A grouped line used to take the last line's unit price, so two flat lines for the same service became one line at a single price and typed amounts were dropped. A grouped line's total is now the sum of the lines it replaces.
- **Settings → Find Services Near Me no longer crashes on Send.** The request screen at the end of that flow needs the app's work-order store. Client mode provided one; Settings didn't.
- **Deleting a zone while editing another no longer crashes the property map.** The map kept the edited zone's old place in the list, so after an earlier zone was deleted the next tap reached past the end of the list.
- **Message on a client is only offered where the device can send texts.** Presenting the message composer without checking fails on an iPad without Messages. Every other screen already checked.
- **Zone slopes are looked up again.** Every lookup went to `api.open-topo-data.com`, which doesn't exist, so every zone silently read as flat. They now go to Open Topo Data's real host, `api.opentopodata.org`, the service the privacy policy names. All of a property's zones go in one request, because the public API allows one request a second. A point it has no data for no longer fails the whole lookup. A lookup that fails keeps the zone's previous grade instead of resetting it to flat.
- **Saving a mapped property replaces its zones only once the new ones are ready.** The saved zones were deleted before the slope lookups ran, so a slow lookup, or the app closing during one, could leave the client with no zones.
- **Slope lookups respect Open Topo Data's one-request-a-second limit across lookups too,** not only within one, so going back and forth on the property screen doesn't get a lookup refused. The coordinates sent are rounded to five decimals, about a metre, instead of full precision; the 30 m elevation data gains nothing from more.
- **Deleting an invoice no longer makes the next one repeat a live invoice's number.** The next number was the count of invoices plus one, worked out separately on four screens: delete INV-0003 of five and the next invoice was a second INV-0005. The next number is now one past the highest already used, from one place (`InvoiceNumbering`). A revision holds its original's number, so revising INV-0007 and then deleting the original doesn't hand INV-0007 out again. The builder numbers an invoice when it's built, so the preview can show it, and takes the next free number at save if that one has been taken meanwhile. Two devices creating invoices before they sync can still pick the same number.
- **Revision numbers work the same way.** Deleting a revision no longer repeats its number, and revising a revision gives INV-0042-R2 rather than INV-0042-R1-R1. The "Revise Paid Invoice" message now names the number the copy will actually get; it always said -R1.
- **Repeating visits fall on the days chosen.** Add Visit created a series with its own date arithmetic, which ignored the weekdays picked: weekly on Monday and Thursday, starting on a Wednesday, gave 52 Wednesdays. Creating a series and continuing it now use one rule (`RecurrenceRule`), and every date is counted from the series' first visit.
- **Completing a repeating visit no longer duplicates the next one.** The series is created up front, but completing any of its visits added "the next one" again, so every completion left a duplicate, and those copies lost the visit's reason and expected services. A series now gets another visit only when nothing after the completed one is still scheduled, dated after the series' latest visit, and carrying everything the series has. Skipping the last visit no longer ends the series, and a skipped visit is never re-created.
- **Visits keep their time of day and day of month.** A visit that wrapped into the next week was placed at midnight, so it showed as overdue all day. Repeating every two or more weeks skipped the week's other chosen days. A monthly series from the 31st slid to the 28th after February and stayed there. A series with a visit at 2:30 AM moved to 3:00 for good after the spring clock change.
- **The end date includes its whole day, and a series is created at most two years ahead,** so "every 30 months" no longer books 52 visits reaching 130 years out.
- **Totals agree everywhere, from one formula.** The builder's "Estimated total" left out tax and custom lines, so it was lower than the document it saved. The invoice Edit screen showed a second "Total" with the discount taken off twice. A document's totals now come from one place (`Proposal.totals`), and tax is rounded to the cent once, so the PDF's rows always add up to its total.
- **Each line is added up at the cent it prints as.** A line holding fractions of a cent, like 10.125, printed as 10.13 but was added up unrounded, so the PDF's rows could miss its total by a cent. Amounts are now rounded to the cent as they're typed, and older lines are added up as printed. An older document with such lines can change by up to a cent per line, including a sent invoice that isn't paid yet. Its new total is the one its rows add up to.
- **Tax rates keep three decimals.** Opening an invoice's Edit screen and tapping Done saved 8.875 % as 8.9 %, because the field showed one decimal, and the PDF printed "Tax (8.9%)". A typed rate is now kept to three decimals, and every rate is shown as it's charged, so the Edit screen, the PDF label and the rate charged agree. A rate typed with four decimals, like 7.0625, is saved as 7.063; one saved that way before is shown and charged as it was.
- **The Edit screen keeps what was changed on it, however it's closed.** Line amounts saved as they were typed, but the discount, tax and due date saved only on Done, so swiping the screen away or tapping Mark as Sent kept some edits and dropped others. All three now save them. Only fields changed on the screen are saved, so opening an older invoice and closing it leaves its amounts exactly as they were, and a change synced from another device meanwhile isn't overwritten. An invoice without a due date gets the one the screen shows.
- **A cleared amount in the builder uses the after-hours price shown beside it,** not the plain catalog price. The price shown, the estimate and the saved document now come from one place (`DocumentDraft`).
- **Payment reminders texted to clients give the amount to the cent.** A $149.50 invoice was texted as $150.
- **The PDF's discount row shows the discount actually taken,** never more than the work it comes off.
- **No signal no longer signs you out.** At every launch PlowR asks Apple whether the sign-in is still valid, and it signed out on any answer but yes, including no answer at all. On a job site without signal, that deleted the saved sign-in and locked the user out of their routes until they had signal to sign in again. Now only Apple saying the sign-in was revoked, isn't known or was transferred signs the user out.
- **Opening PlowR after it started with the phone locked no longer shows the sign-in screen.** Started in the background while the phone was locked, for example by a geofence, it couldn't read the saved sign-in, and it didn't look again when opened. It now checks again whenever it comes to the front signed out.
- **Terrain lookups stay one request a second, even on a busy phone.** The gap between requests was measured from when each was booked, not when it went, so a wait that ended late let the next request follow at once and could trip the free API's rate limit. The gap is now measured from when the previous request actually went, and checked again after every wait. A test that measured this failed twice on the CI machine, with requests 0.4 ms and 54 ms apart.
- **Delete Account & Data removes everything PlowR keeps on the device.** It left behind the client-mode name, phone and email, pending notifications (the daily overdue-invoice reminder kept firing), geofences from a route the app was killed during, shared invoice and report PDFs, and archived copies of the database. It now removes all of them, and every preference rather than a list of known ones, so one added later can't be missed. If a step fails, Settings says what wasn't removed and the account stays signed in so it can be tried again; before, every step failed silently and the user was signed out as if it had worked. The iCloud copy isn't removed by the app; the confirmation says how to remove it in the Settings app.
- **The home-screen widget no longer says "Stop 9 of 8".** After the last stop, until the route was ended, it counted one stop past the end and showed "All stops complete" as the next client. It now says "All 8 stops done" with no next stop.
- **Inactive clients are no longer put on new routes.** The 1.1.0 notes said they are hidden from route building, but the New Route and Edit Route pickers listed them with every other client, Schedule's Create Route added them when a visit was still scheduled from before they were marked inactive, and duplicating a route copied them. All four now leave inactive clients out. Create Route says how many visits it left off, and if none are left it says so instead of saving an empty route. A stop already on a route stays until it's removed.
- **Routes show their progress.** Completing a stop never recorded its time, so the route list and the dashboard never showed how many stops were done, the route map never marked a stop done, and the recap called every stop without recorded services "Not Serviced". Each stop now records its time when it's completed, at least a minute. Starting a route clears the last run's times, recorded services and notes, so last week's work doesn't show as done and last week's note isn't pre-filled onto this week's invoice; picking a route back up after a relaunch keeps them. A route is shown as in progress only while it's running, not whenever some of its stops are done.
- **A database that can't be migrated is moved aside instead of stopping the app at every launch.** PlowR's last resort archived the store from Application Support and started a new one, but on a signed build the store is in the app-group container, so nothing moved, the retry failed the same way, and the app stopped at launch until it was reinstalled. It now archives the store from where it really is, with the folder of photos kept beside it, and Delete Account & Data removes those archives too.
- **Only a store that really can't be migrated is moved aside.** Moving it for any failure to open, such as a phone still locked after a restart, would hide the user's data behind an empty app. Now a store is archived only when Core Data says it can't be migrated to the current model, even with iCloud off. Anything else, such as a full disk, stops with the data in place, as before, so a fixed build still finds it. Moves are all or nothing.
- **"Lawn Service" and "Tree Service" no longer show a snowflake.** The route screen and the proposal PDF picked a service's icon by looking for keywords anywhere in its name, so "service" matched "ice" and "street" matched "tree". A keyword now has to start a word, and both screens use the same rule; "Salting" and "De-icing" still get the ice icon.
- **PlowR says when your data isn't backed up to iCloud.** A card at the top of the Dashboard, and the Data & Backup row in Settings, say so when PlowR can't use iCloud on this device (not signed in, or iCloud off for PlowR), when iCloud is restricted, when the Apple Account needs attention, and when PlowR couldn't open its iCloud database at launch. In the first three cases clients, routes and documents are only on this device. PlowR used to warn only in that last, rare case: someone not signed in to iCloud was never told, and Settings kept saying "Data Backed Up to iCloud". Settings now says that only when iCloud has said so. The card can be closed; it comes back for a different problem, or for the same one after iCloud has worked in between. It replaces an alert that came back every time the Dashboard opened. When iCloud can't answer, the Dashboard shows nothing: there's nothing for the user to fix.
- Delete Account & Data removes the PlowR calendar and every event PlowR added, with client names and addresses. It used to leave them. If PlowR's calendar access has been turned off in the Settings app, PlowR can't reach the calendar: Delete Account says so, and how to remove it, instead of reporting everything gone.
- **Save as Preset on Add Visit saves only a real reason.** Typing "Other" and saving it as a preset picked the picker's own "Other…" entry, which left the visit with no reason. Save as Preset now shows only for a reason that isn't blank, isn't "Other" and isn't listed already, and it drops the spaces around what was typed, so "Inspection " doesn't become a second "Inspection".
- **Siri's "Complete current stop in PlowR" completes the stop you're at**, without opening the app and whether or not the route screen is showing, and says what happened: which stop is next, that it was the last one, or that no route is in progress. It used to reach the stop only through the route screen while it was on screen, opened the notify prompt instead of completing, did nothing on the last stop, and said "Stop marked complete." either way. App Review checks that advertised features work (guideline 2.1).
- **Control Center's Complete Stop shows the client whose stop it completes, and completes it.** It opens PlowR, which marks that stop done if it's still the current one and shows the next; otherwise the route screen says why. A second tap before the control has caught up says the stop is already done. It used to only open the app, while its description promised more.
- Neither completes a stop you haven't seen. If iCloud removed the stop you were at, while PlowR was closed or in the background, the route moves to the next one. Siri waits until the route screen has shown it, even if iOS closed PlowR in between. Control Center completes only the stop it showed, so it refuses the removed one. Completing it straight away would have credited that client with a visit that didn't happen. A completion is also saved at once, not left to autosave, which a phone can suspend before it runs.
- The route screen's service recorder stays on the stop it was opened for. If the stop was completed while the recorder was open, saving used to put this client's services, photos and invoice on the next stop. The notify sheet likewise no longer says "changed on another device" when its stop was completed by Siri. A stop's job-site geofence now follows the route, whoever completes the stop.
- **Cancelling a text no longer counts as sending it.**
  - Messaging a route's clients one at a time: cancelling one client's text opened the next client's, with no way to stop and no record of who had been sent it. Now each client drops out of the selection as their text goes, and a cancelled or failed text stops the run and says how many were sent. The clients not texted yet stay selected, so Send carries on with them, and while the screen is open no one is texted twice. Each text now has to be sent or cancelled; it can't be swiped away.
  - A group text that's cancelled leaves the Message Clients screen open. It used to close it as if the text had gone.
  - The notify prompt: cancelling the text marked the stop done and moved on as if the text had gone. Now it goes back to the prompt, where Skip moves on without a text.
- **Siri's "Notify next client" only opens a text, to your current stop's client, and marks nothing complete.** It used to open the route screen's prompt, whose Send and Skip also mark the current stop done: said after "Complete current stop", it offered to text the client after next and marked a stop not yet reached as done. Say "Complete current stop" first, then "Notify next client"; Siri names the client ("your current stop") and the prompt reads "Text Current Stop?". It also works when Siri opens PlowR, not only when the route screen was already showing, and a request that waited behind another screen is dropped if that stop has been completed meanwhile, or after two minutes.
- **Route stops follow their client.** A client's name, phone, address and map pin were copied onto each route stop when it was made and never again, so a corrected phone number or address, or a pin set by hand, never reached the routes the client was already on: the stop kept texting the old number, and the map, drive times and job-site alerts used the old house. Now a change on the client's page, with Adjust Pin or with the property scanner's Move Pin updates their stops on every route, and so does a change that iCloud brings from another device. On a route in progress, the current stop's job-site alert moves with its pin. Stops that had already fallen behind catch up when PlowR opens or comes back to the front. A stop's own notes and what a run recorded are left as they were.
- **Edit Client keeps a pin set with Adjust Pin or Move Pin.** Tapping Save afterwards wrote back the address the screen had opened with, and an address typed before could be looked up again over the pin just set by hand.
- **An address the map can't place is no longer saved silently.** Adding a client (by hand or from a scanned business card), or changing a client's address, when the address can't be put on the map now asks: save without a map pin (or, for a client who already has one, keep it), or edit the address. If the map couldn't be reached (no signal, say), it says so and offers Try Again.
  - Adding a client used to save them with no pin, which leaves them off route maps, with no drive times and no job-site alerts, and nothing said so.
  - Changing the address used to keep the old address's pin without asking, so a client who had moved stayed at their old house on every map and route.
  - Edit Client now saves a new address only once it's been looked up, and can't be left or changed while that runs.
- **A client with no pin can be given one.** Their page has "Set Pin on the Map". The map opens where you are, or around your other clients, and the pin can be confirmed once you've zoomed in to the house. A pin set by hand keeps the address as typed, even one typed just before. "Adjust Pin" used to be hidden for these clients, so the missing pin couldn't be fixed.
- **Adjust Pin saves only when the pin moved.** Opening it looked the pin's address up again, and Confirm wrote that back, often reformatted or a neighbour's number, over the client's address.
- **A picked address suggestion keeps its pin.** The address field threw it away as soon as it filled in, so saving looked the address up again, and the suggestions came back.
- **Deleting a client takes them off their routes and the schedule.** Their route stops stayed, though the prompt said they'd be removed, and so did their visits. The prompt now says which routes they're on, and that their upcoming visits and their photos go with them (photos are shown only on the client's page). It asks whether to keep their invoices, proposals and past visits for your records or delete everything, and offers to mark them Inactive instead.
- **Back on a client's page asks before dropping unsaved changes.** It offers Save or Discard Changes, and swiping back from the edge doesn't leave while there are changes. It used to drop every edit without a word: a corrected phone number, new notes or a changed address were simply gone. Save still needs a name and a phone number. A client changed elsewhere while the page is open (scheduling a visit makes an inactive client active; an edit syncs from another device) no longer makes the page look edited, and its Save no longer writes the old values back. Tapping the Clients tab again while the page is open still leaves without asking.
- **A new business profile keeps the app's navy for proposals and invoices.** The colour picker started at system blue, so saving a new profile for the first time wrote blue over the navy default.
- **The profile's accent colour no longer shifts when saved.** It was stored as hex with each channel rounded down, so some colours came back a step darker after a save. A colour picked outside the standard range (Display P3) was stored as hex that couldn't be read back, so the form showed the default.

### Internal
- **CI has been red since 2026-08-27** because the host app died before the test runner could connect to it. `PlowRApp.makeContainer` built its `ModelContainer` with `cloudKitDatabase:` unconditionally, and the `try?` around it cannot catch a CloudKit failure: CoreData accepts the configuration, then sets CloudKit up asynchronously on `com.apple.coredata.cloudkit.queue` and **traps** rather than throwing. GitHub runners are signed out of iCloud and the workflow builds with `CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO`, so there is no iCloud entitlement at all — the app trapped a few seconds after launch, surfacing as "Test crashed with signal trap before establishing connection." CloudKit mirroring is now skipped when `XCTestConfigurationFilePath` is set, falling through to the existing local store. Tests should not be syncing to a real iCloud database regardless. Confirmed by CI: the `Tests` run on #1 passed, the first green run since 2026-08-27.
- **Product → Archive no longer edits the project.** The PlowR target had an Archive-only Run Script that ran `agvtool next-version -all` in the project folder, so every local archive rewrote `CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION` in the tracked `project.pbxproj` (1 → 2 on all four build configurations, observed on a test archive of `main` on 2026-09-26) and left a diff nobody had typed. Xcode Cloud, which PlowR is moving to, numbers builds itself and ignores the file's value, so the script had no job left. An archive of this branch leaves every tracked file unchanged and takes its build number from `CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION` as written.
- **The placeholder `example()` test is gone; a real one replaces it.** It asserted nothing and was counted as a passing test. `ModelContainerTests` now checks that a launch under test used the local store rather than CloudKit (`PlowRApp.isCloudKitAvailable` is false), pinning the CI fix above. Verified by breaking it on purpose: with the fallback no longer recording itself, the test fails. A second assertion, that the test run is detected at all, was tried and dropped. If detection breaks, the app traps on launch before any assertion runs, so that check could never fail on its own. The crash is already the red signal for that regression.
- **`docs/ci.md` documents PlowR's CI and the one-time Xcode Cloud setup.** Xcode Cloud workflows live only in App Store Connect, so without this file the definition of what gates `main` would exist nowhere in the repo. Two workflows: `CI Tests` on PRs to `main`, and `Release Flow` (manual start, `main` only, distribution "TestFlight and App Store", TestFlight internal post-action). Both are pinned to Xcode 26.6 / macOS 26.5.1. The file includes numbered setup steps. - **Xcode Cloud `CI Tests` and `Release Flow` exist.** Both were created on 2026-09-26 from the steps in `docs/ci.md`, and its Status table now says so. Release Flow has not been started; its first run will be PlowR's first Release Flow build. Before removing it, Xcode Cloud had to show it really gates: its first run went green on #7, and a throwaway PR that broke the app on purpose (#8, closed unmerged) came back with 1 test failure and a failed `PlowR | CI Tests`.
- **`scripts/test.sh` is the way to run the tests.** It forwards to a shared toolkit outside the repo, configured by `.claude/app.json`, so the same hardened runner serves every app without one repo depending on another. The verdict needs xcodebuild's exit code, its `** TEST SUCCEEDED **` line and the result bundle together. Counts come from the bundle, not the log. A run of 0 tests is a failure: a test filter that matches nothing still makes xcodebuild print TEST SUCCEEDED. `.gitignore` keeps the rest of `.claude/` (local settings, worktrees) out of git.
- **A service-language guard runs on every PR.** `.github/workflows/guards.yml` (Linux, cheap) fails when snow-only wording ("Start Plowing", "Snow Route") or a fixed snowflake icon appears on a screen every industry shares: dashboard, active route, routes, Live Activity, intents, widgets, tab bar. 1.1.0 removed exactly that kind of copy; nothing stopped it coming back. Snow-specific places (the service catalog, weather, icons chosen from a service's category) are deliberately not checked. Verified both ways: the current code passes, and a planted "Start Plowing" or snowflake icon fails.
- **`Versions.xcconfig` is the one place version numbers live.** They were project-level build settings, and the test target overrode them with a stale `MARKETING_VERSION = 1.0`. Now the project's Debug and Release configurations are based on the xcconfig and no target sets its own values. Every target resolves to 1.1.0 (1), as the app and widget did before. Verified in the built product: with the file temporarily set to 9.9.9 (42), an archive's app and widget `Info.plist` both read 9.9.9 (42).
- **PRs open with a template** that asks what changed and why, how each claim was verified (read, inspected, ran, or broke on purpose), and what's left untested. It ends with a reminder that the repo is public.
- **`DesignSystem.swift` holds the brand colours.** The navy was hand-copied in four places: the app tint, the client-list avatar, the PDF default accent, and `BusinessProfile`'s default hex. They now all read `PlowRColor`, with the same values, so nothing looks different. The file is also compiled into the widget extension, ready for the widget and Live Activity to adopt. Two tests guard the parts that could silently drift: the navy's hex string and RGB values must be the same colour, and the accent must be that navy in light mode and the shipped bright blue (0.53, 0.70, 1.00) in dark mode. Each was broken on purpose and failed. `PlowRColor` is `nonisolated`: SwiftData's generated model code reads `navyHex` off the main actor, which strict concurrency flags as an error-to-be in Swift 6.
- **SwiftLint runs on every PR, report-only for now.** `.swiftlint.yml` targets crash-class problems (force unwraps, force casts, `try!`) and turns off the size and style rules that would bury them. The first run found 118 violations, 35 at error severity, all `force_unwrapping`, in 9 files. Until those are fixed, the `SwiftLint (report only)` job can't block a merge, and its name says so, so a green tick isn't read as a clean lint. `scripts/lint.sh` runs the same check locally and aborts unless its exclusions match `.claude/app.json`.
- **`main` is protected.** A ruleset blocks deletion and force-pushes, allows changes only through a squash-merged PR, and requires the Xcode Cloud tests and the service-language guard to pass. Nobody is on the bypass list. It was first saved as "Disabled", which GitHub shows much like an active ruleset, so enforcement was confirmed from the API's list of rules active on `main`, not from the settings page. `docs/ci.md` and `CLAUDE.md` record it.
- **No force-unwraps left (35 → 0).** SwiftLint found 35 `!` in 9 files. Read one by one, none could crash with today's code: each sat next to a guard (a list checked non-empty a few lines up, a dictionary key taken from that same dictionary). They would have become crashes the day someone moved the guard. All 35 are gone. The map framing was hand-written four times (route map, route recap, client property map, the proposal PDF's minimap) and is now one `CoordinateBounds` type whose failable initializer makes the empty case impossible to forget. Its 5 tests pin its arithmetic and defaults; each was broken on purpose and failed. The route-optimizer loop was rewritten to track its current stop, and review showed the existing tests couldn't tell whether that stop was ever updated. A new 4-stop test can, and fails when the update is removed.
- **SwiftLint now blocks merges.** With the force-unwraps at 0 (#17), the job lost `continue-on-error` and its "(report only)" label, and "SwiftLint" joined the required checks on `main`. A new force unwrap, force cast or `try!` now fails the PR instead of passing with a green tick.

The repo's "Allow auto-merge" setting was turned on the same day.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-08-22

### Added
- **Live Activity** on Lock Screen and Dynamic Island during active routes — shows route name, stop progress, and next client name in real time
- **Home screen widget** (small + medium sizes) with three states: active route with progress bar and next stop details, route complete summary, and idle placeholder; tapping opens directly to Routes tab
- **Control Center button** (iOS 18+) — mark the current stop complete from Control Center without unlocking the app
- **Haptic feedback** throughout the active route flow: starting, advancing stops, opening the notify prompt, and completing the full route
- **Dashboard weather** — current conditions shown on the home screen using the device's last known location, no extra permission needed
- **Local weather alert notifications** — fires at 6 PM the evening before any forecast day with snow, heavy rain, or thunderstorms
- **Overdue invoice reminder notifications** — daily 9 AM alert when outstanding overdue invoices exist
- **Calendar sync** — scheduled visits are automatically added to a "PlowR" calendar in Calendar.app with a 1-hour alarm; removals sync on delete
- **Siri Shortcuts** — "Complete current stop" and "Notify next client" intents available in Shortcuts and via voice
- **Route optimization — two modes**: Driving Distance (real MKDirections nearest-neighbor routing) and Quick Optimize (instant straight-line haversine)
- **Geofencing** — automatically detects when you drive away from a stop and prompts you to notify the next client
- **App Store review prompt** — appears after completing 2 or more routes with at least 5 stops
- **Camera scan-to-client** — scan a business card or handwritten note with the camera to auto-fill a new client using Vision OCR
- **Inactive client support** — clients can be marked inactive and are hidden from route building but preserved in history
- **Mass message sending** — compose and send a message to all clients on the current route at once
- **Route recap screen** — full summary of completed stops, services rendered, and total time shown at route end
- **Dashboard action tiles** — quick-tap shortcuts for common actions directly from the home screen

### Changed
- Service language and icons are now industry-agnostic, covering snow removal, lawn care, and landscaping throughout the app
- Route optimize button now offers a choice of optimization method rather than running automatically

### Fixed
- Share sheet no longer appears multiple times on repeated taps
- Swipe-to-delete now works correctly in lists where it was previously unresponsive
- Saving forms now dismisses the sheet as expected instead of requiring a manual close
- Three tester-reported stability issues resolved (stop advance crash, address field blur, schedule date persistence)

### Security
- Apple Sign In user ID moved from UserDefaults to Keychain (`kSecAttrAccessibleWhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly`)
- Client work orders (address, phone, notes) migrated from UserDefaults to an encrypted file (`completeFileProtection`) in Application Support
- Location-sharing toggle in notify prompt now defaults to off; preference is remembered across prompts
- Weather API calls now use rounded coordinates (~1 km precision) rather than precise GPS to minimize location data transmitted to Open-Meteo
- App data store recovery path now archives (renames) the stale database instead of deleting it silently, preventing unrecoverable data loss
- Dashboard now shows a warning banner if iCloud sync is unavailable so operators know data is stored locally only
- Camera usage description updated to accurately disclose both photo capture and business card OCR scan uses
- `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` manifest added, declaring UserDefaults (required-reason CA92.1) and precise-location data transmitted to Open-Meteo for weather
